หัวข้อ 1 — Greetings 인사말

Greetings in Korean 한국어 인사말

Korean greetings change depending on who you are talking to and the situation. The most important distinction is formal vs. casual speech. Use formal forms with strangers, elders, and in professional settings. Casual forms are for friends and people younger than you.

💡 Key rule: when in doubt, be formal

Koreans appreciate effort. Using the formal 하세요 ending is always safe with people you just met. Switching to casual 안녕 with a close friend shows warmth.

หัวข้อ 2 — Numbers 숫자

Two Number Systems 두 가지 숫자 체계

Korean uses two separate number systems: Native Korean numbers (고유어) and Sino-Korean numbers (한자어). You must know both — they are used in different situations.

  • Native Korean: use for counting items (개), age (살), and hours (시) — e.g., 하나, 둘, 셋
  • Sino-Korean: use for money (원), minutes (분), dates, phone numbers, and addresses — e.g., 일, 이, 삼
  • Large numbers always use Sino-Korean — 백 (100), 천 (1,000), 만 (10,000)

🔢 Quick memory trick

Think: Native = natural body rhythms (heartbeat, age, counting on your fingers). Sino = systematic/official contexts (clocks, money, calendars).

Sino-Korean Numbers 한자어 숫자

Sino-Korean numbers come from Chinese (한자, hanja). They are used for money, minutes, dates, phone numbers, floors of buildings, and large quantities.

  • 일(1), 이(2), 삼(3), 사(4), 오(5), 육(6), 칠(7), 팔(8), 구(9), 십(10)
  • 백 (100), 천 (1,000), 만 (10,000)
  • Price: 오천 원 (5,000 won), 이만 원 (20,000 won)

Korean Counters 단위 명사

Korean numbers must be paired with counters (단위명사) — special words that indicate what kind of thing you are counting. The counter comes after the number.

  • 개 (gae) — general objects: 사과 두 개 (two apples)
  • 명 (myeong) — people (formal): 학생 세 명 (three students)
  • 살 (sal) — age: 스물다섯 살 (25 years old)
  • 권 (gwon) — books: 책 한 권 (one book)
  • 잔 (jan) — cups / glasses: 커피 한 잔 (one cup of coffee)
  • 번 (beon) — number order / times: 두 번 (two times)

หัวข้อ 3 — Family 가족

Family in Korean 가족

Korean family vocabulary is more detailed than English — it distinguishes not just the relationship, but whether you are older or younger, and whether you are male or female. For example, 'older brother' has different words depending on whether you are a boy (형) or a girl (오빠).

👨‍👩‍👧 Gender matters here

Male speakers use 형 (older brother) and 누나 (older sister). Female speakers use 오빠 (older brother) and 언니 (older sister). This is one of Korean's most distinctive features.

หัวข้อ 4 — Food & Drink 음식과 음료

Korean Food & Drink 한국 음식과 음료

Korean cuisine is built around rice, fermented vegetables, and shared communal dishes. Food vocabulary will immediately come up at restaurants, markets, and when ordering delivery — a deeply ingrained part of Korean daily life.

🍜 맛있어요 (mashisseoyo)

The most important food phrase: 맛있어요 = It's delicious! Use it freely and often — Korean hosts love hearing it.

หัวข้อ 5 — Colors 색깔

Colors in Korean 색깔

Korean has two ways to express colors. Descriptive adjectives end in -다 (e.g., 빨갛다 = to be red) and are used in sentences. Color nouns end in -색 (color) and are used to name the color itself. Both forms are widely used.

🎨 -색 vs. adjective form

빨간색 (the color red) vs. 빨개요 (it is red). For naming a color outright, use the -색 form. For describing something, use the adjective form: 이 셔츠는 빨개요 (This shirt is red).

หัวข้อ 6 — Days & Time 요일과 시간

Days & Time in Korean 요일과 시간

Korean days of the week come from the Chinese five-element system plus sun and moon. Hours use native Korean numbers (하나~열), while minutes use Sino-Korean numbers (일~오십구). Master this split and you can tell any time in Korean.

  • Days: 월(Mon), 화(Tue), 수(Wed), 목(Thu), 금(Fri), 토(Sat), 일(Sun) + 요일
  • Hours: use native Korean numbers → 한 시 (1 o'clock), 두 시 (2 o'clock)
  • Minutes: use Sino-Korean numbers → 오 분 (5 min), 삼십 분 (30 min)
  • AM/PM: 오전 (morning/AM), 오후 (afternoon/PM)

🗓️ Memory trick for days

월 = Moon (月), 화 = Fire (火), 수 = Water (水), 목 = Wood (木), 금 = Gold (金), 토 = Earth (土), 일 = Sun (日) — the same as Japanese days!

Telling Time in Korean 시간 말하기

To tell time: say AM/PM + hour (native number + 시) + minute (Sino-Korean number + 분). Example: 오전 두 시 삼십 분 = 2:30 AM. 오후 다섯 시 = 5:00 PM.

หัวข้อ 7 — Places 장소

Places in Korean 장소

Knowing place names lets you navigate cities, ask for directions, and tell people where you are going. These words are also key building blocks in Korean sentences — many verbs are built around locations.

📍 Asking where something is

[Place] 어디예요? = Where is the [place]? E.g., 화장실 어디예요? = Where is the restroom? Simple pattern, huge daily utility.

หัวข้อ 13 — Verbs 동사

Korean Verbs 동사

Korean verbs (동사) always come at the end of a sentence. The dictionary form ends in -다. To make the polite present tense, change -다 to -아요 or -어요 depending on the vowel in the final syllable. Example: 먹다 (to eat) → 먹어요 (I eat / eats).

🔤 Verb conjugation rule

Last syllable has ㅏ or ㅗ vowel → use -아요 (e.g., 가다 → 가요). All other vowels → use -어요 (e.g., 먹다 → 먹어요). Verbs ending in 하다 → 해요 (e.g., 공부하다 → 공부해요).

หัวข้อ 14 — Adjectives 형용사

Korean Adjectives 형용사

Korean adjectives (형용사) behave differently from English ones — they conjugate like verbs. The dictionary form ends in -다. To use them as predicates, change -다 to -아요/어요. To modify a noun, change -다 to -(으)ㄴ. Example: 크다 (big) → 커요 (it's big) / 큰 집 (big house).

Dictionary form: 크다 (big). Statement: 커요 (it's big). Noun-modifier: 큰 집 (big house). This pattern applies to almost all Korean adjectives — master the pattern, and every adjective you learn doubles its use.

หัวข้อ 18 — Proverbs 속담

Korean Proverbs (속담) 한국 속담

Korean proverbs (속담) offer a window into the values, wisdom, and humor of Korean culture. These sayings have been passed down for centuries and are still used in everyday conversation. Understanding them will make you sound like a native — and reveal the soul of the language.

🌿 How Koreans use proverbs

Koreans drop proverbs into conversation to make a point concisely. When someone works incredibly hard, you might hear: 낮말은 새가 듣고 밤말은 쥐가 들어 (Birds hear your day words; mice hear your night words — be careful what you say). Mastering even 5 proverbs will impress any Korean speaker.

가는 말이 고와야 오는 말이 곱다 가는 말이 고와야 오는 말이 곱다

Meaning: If your words going out are kind, the words coming back will be kind. English equivalent: 'You reap what you sow.'

  • 가는 말 = words going out / what you say
  • 오는 말 = words coming back / what you receive
  • 곱다 = to be beautiful / kind (speech)
  • Lesson: treat others as you want to be treated

호랑이도 제 말 하면 온다 호랑이도 제 말 하면 온다

Meaning: Even a tiger will appear if you talk about it. English equivalent: 'Speak of the devil and he shall appear.'

  • 호랑이 = tiger (the feared animal in Korean folklore)
  • 제 말 하면 = if you talk about it
  • 온다 = comes / appears
  • Used when someone appears right after you mention their name

백지장도 맞들면 낫다 백지장도 맞들면 낫다

Meaning: Even a sheet of white paper is better lifted together. English equivalent: 'Two heads are better than one' / 'Many hands make light work.'

  • 백지장 = sheet of white paper
  • 맞들면 = if you lift it together
  • 낫다 = to be better
  • Emphasizes collaboration over individual effort

천 리 길도 한 걸음부터 천 리 길도 한 걸음부터

Meaning: Even a journey of a thousand li (Korean miles) begins with a single step. Korean equivalent of the famous Lao Tzu saying.

  • 천 리 = one thousand li (Korean unit of distance, ~400 km)
  • 한 걸음 = one step
  • 부터 = starting from
  • Used to encourage someone starting a big goal

낮말은 새가 듣고 밤말은 쥐가 듣는다 낮말은 새가 듣고 밤말은 쥐가 듣는다

Meaning: Birds hear your day words; mice hear your night words. Always speak carefully — there are no truly secret conversations.

  • 낮말 = day words / things said in daytime
  • 밤말 = night words / things said at night
  • 새 = bird | 쥐 = mouse/rat
  • Reminder: be careful what you say about others

고생 끝에 낙이 온다 고생 끝에 낙이 온다

Meaning: After hardship comes joy. English equivalent: 'Every cloud has a silver lining' / 'No pain, no gain.'

  • 고생 = hardship / suffering
  • 끝에 = at the end of
  • 낙 = joy / pleasure / reward
  • A very common expression of encouragement in Korean

빈 수레가 요란하다 빈 수레가 요란하다

Meaning: An empty cart makes the most noise. English equivalent: 'Empty vessels make the most noise' / 'All bark, no bite.'

  • 빈 = empty
  • 수레 = cart / wagon
  • 요란하다 = to be noisy / loud
  • Said about boastful or loud people who lack substance

세 살 버릇 여든까지 간다 세 살 버릇 여든까지 간다

Meaning: Habits learned at age three last until eighty. English equivalent: 'Old habits die hard' / 'As the twig is bent, so grows the tree.'

  • 세 살 = three years old
  • 버릇 = habit
  • 여든 = eighty
  • 까지 = until; 간다 = goes
  • Often said to explain why upbringing matters

원숭이도 나무에서 떨어진다 원숭이도 나무에서 떨어진다

Meaning: Even a monkey falls from a tree. English equivalent: 'Nobody's perfect' / 'Even the best make mistakes.'

  • 원숭이 = monkey
  • 나무에서 = from a tree
  • 떨어진다 = falls
  • Said to comfort someone who made a mistake at something they're good at

급할수록 돌아가라 급할수록 돌아가라

Meaning: The more urgent it is, the more you should take the roundabout way. English equivalent: 'More haste, less speed' / 'Haste makes waste.'

  • 급할수록 = the more urgent it is
  • 돌아가라 = go around / take the longer route
  • The wisdom: slow down when under pressure
  • Used when someone is rushing and making errors

아는 길도 물어가라 아는 길도 물어가라

Meaning: Even on a path you know, ask for directions. English equivalent: 'Better safe than sorry' / 'There's no harm in asking.'

  • 아는 길 = a path you know
  • 물어가라 = ask (your way)
  • Encourages checking even when confident
  • Values humility and double-checking

하늘이 무너져도 솟아날 구멍이 있다 하늘이 무너져도 솟아날 구멍이 있다

Meaning: Even if the sky collapses, there will be a hole to escape through. English equivalent: 'Every cloud has a silver lining' / 'Where there's a will, there's a way.'

  • 하늘이 무너져도 = even if the sky collapses
  • 솟아날 = that you can escape through
  • 구멍 = hole / opening
  • Strong expression of hope in the worst circumstances

คำตอบ: No pain, no gain (No pain, no gain · Empty vessels make noise · Speak of the devil · Every step counts)

คำตอบ: Even experts make mistakes (Monkeys are dangerous · Even experts make mistakes · Practice makes perfect · Avoid tall trees)

คำตอบ: Start with the first step (Walk faster · Start with the first step · Plan everything carefully · Ask for help)

หัวข้อ 19 — Academic 학업

Academic Korean 학업 한국어

Korea places enormous emphasis on education — 교육열 (educational fever) drives students to study intensely. University entrance exams (수능) are a national event. Whether you are a student, teacher, or lifelong learner, this vocabulary will help you navigate academic settings in Korean.

📚 Education culture

수능 (Suneung) is Korea's college entrance exam, held every November — the entire country adapts its schedule around it. Airlines delay flights so exam halls aren't disturbed. It's the most culturally significant academic event in Korea.

Reading or watching Korean news is an excellent way to build advanced vocabulary and stay connected to Korean society. Korean news uses formal speech levels (합쇼체) and many Sino-Korean terms. Understanding these words will help you follow Korean news programs, podcasts, and newspapers.

หัวข้อ 21 — Konglish 콩글리시

Konglish — Korean + English 콩글리시

Konglish (콩글리시) refers to English loanwords adapted into Korean. These words are pronounced differently and sometimes have different meanings from the original English. Learning Konglish is a fast track to a large vocabulary, since many modern Korean words are derived from English.

🔤 Sound rules

English consonant clusters are broken up with vowels: 'stress' → 스트레스 (seuteureseu). Final consonants get a vowel added: 'cake' → 케이크 (keikeu). 'r' and 'l' both become ㄹ: 'rice' → 라이스, 'lemon' → 레몬.
