🔢 Numbers (숫자)

ℹ️
Two Number Systems
Korean has Native Korean numbers (for counting, age, hours) and Sino-Korean numbers (for money, dates, minutes, phone numbers). You need both!

💡
When to use each system
Native Korean: age (살), hours (시), counting items (개), floors (층) — e.g., 스물다섯 살 (25 years old). Sino-Korean: money (원), minutes (분), dates, phone numbers, addresses — e.g., 이만 원 (20,000 won).

🌐 What is Konglish? 콩글리시란?
콩글리시 (Konglish)
한국어(Korean) + 영어(English) = 콩글리시
Words that look and sound like English — but carry completely different meanings in Korea. Koreans borrowed English words and gave them new Korean lives!
⚠️
Warning for English Speakers
Using these words with their original English meanings in Korea will cause confusion! For example, saying "컨센트" (consent) in a hardware store means you need an electrical outlet — not that you're agreeing to something.
