Stage 1 · Shopping Vocabulary 쇼핑 단어

Shopping in Korea (쇼핑)

Korea has a rich shopping culture — from traditional outdoor markets (시장) to high-end department stores (백화점) to 24-hour convenience stores (편의점) on every corner. Bargaining is normal at traditional markets but not at chain stores or malls. Learning to ask prices, compare items, and complete transactions in Korean will make your shopping experience much smoother and more personal. The key question you'll use constantly is 얼마예요? (How much is it?)

🛒 Gwangjang Market vs department store

At traditional markets like 광장시장 (Gwangjang Market) in Seoul, you can bargain — especially if buying multiple items. At 롯데백화점 (Lotte Department Store) or any chain, prices are fixed. Watch for 세일 (sale) signs and 할인 (discount) promotions.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
가게ga-gestore / shop
시장si-jangmarket (traditional outdoor)
백화점baek-hwa-jeomdepartment store

Check your understanding

It is 2 a.m. and you need a drink. Which word is the 24-hour shop on the corner?
Answer: 편의점 (가게 · 백화점 · 시장 · 편의점)

Shopping Item Words (쇼핑 물건)

Korean uses many loanwords for modern products — 핸드폰 (cell phone), 노트북 (laptop), 티셔츠 (T-shirt). Native Korean and Sino-Korean words cover traditional items: 옷 (clothes), 신발 (shoes), 가방 (bag). When shopping, you'll often point to something and say 이거 (this one) — a simple and effective strategy. Korean has three of these pointing words: 이거 (this one, near you), 그거 (that one, near the person you are talking to) and 저거 (that one over there, away from you both). Sizes are often given in free-size (프리 사이즈), small (S), medium (M), and large (L/XL).

👕 이거 주세요 — your shopping lifeline

이거 주세요 (I'll take this one, please) + 얼마예요? (How much?) will get you through most Korean shopping scenarios even if you know little else. Point, say these two phrases, and you're set.

Stage 2 · In the Store 가게에서

Key Shopping Phrases (쇼핑 표현)

A few essential phrases cover almost every shopping interaction. 얼마예요? (How much is it?) works for any price enquiry. 이거 주세요 (Please give me this) completes a purchase. 더 싸게 해 주세요 (Please make it cheaper) is used for bargaining at traditional markets. 있어요? (Do you have...?) + the item name asks if something is in stock. 없어요 means it's out of stock or unavailable.

🗣️ Polite shopping tone

In Korean shops, staff will often greet you with 어서 오세요! (Welcome!). Respond with a nod or 안녕하세요. When you've finished shopping, 감사합니다 (thank you) is always appreciated. Shouting across the store is rare — get close before speaking.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
얼마예요eol-ma-ye-yoHow much is it?
이거 주세요i-geo ju-se-yoPlease give me this / I'll take this
더 싸게 해 주세요deo ssa-ge hae ju-se-yoPlease make it cheaper / Can you give me a discount?

Check your understanding

The item is already in your hand and you have decided to buy it. Which do you say?
Answer: 이거 주세요 (이거 주세요 · 그거 주세요 · 저거 주세요 · 이거 얼마예요?)

Asking About Availability (있어요? / 없어요)

있어요? (Is there? / Do you have?) and 없어요 (There isn't / We don't have it) are two of the most useful words in Korean shopping. Attach an item before 있어요?: 이거 있어요? (Do you have this?), 더 큰 사이즈 있어요? (Do you have a bigger size?). 없어요 is the reply if it's sold out. You can also say 다 팔렸어요 (All sold out) in more detail.

📦 있다 vs 없다

있다 = to exist / to have. 없다 = to not exist / to not have. 있어요? alone at the end of a sentence = Do you have...? 없어요. = We don't. These two words appear in almost every Korean sentence that discusses possessing, location, or availability.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
있어요iss-eo-yoThere is / I have / We have (polite)
없어요eop-seo-yoThere isn't / We don't have it / Sold out

Check your understanding

You ask for a bigger size. The clerk checks the back and comes back empty-handed. Which reply do you hear?
Answer: 없어요 (있어요 · 없어요 · 알겠어요 · 아니에요)

Stage 3 · Prices & Numbers 가격·숫자

Korean Money & Prices (원) 원·가격

The Korean currency is the 원 (won, ₩). Prices can look large because 1,000 won ≈ 0.75 USD. A coffee might be 4,500원, a meal 8,000~12,000원. 비싸다 (expensive) and 싸다 (cheap) are the key opinion words. When you receive change, the amount back is 거스름돈. Prices at traditional markets are often negotiable — 깎아 주세요 (Please give me a discount) or 더 싸게요? (Can it be cheaper?) opens bargaining.

💰 Reading Korean prices

Korean uses Sino-Korean numbers for prices: 일(1) 이(2) 삼(3) 사(4) 오(5). 만 = 10,000. So 삼만 오천 원 = 35,000 won. Cashiers often show you the amount on a calculator or screen to avoid confusion — pointing at numbers works perfectly well.

Stage 4 · Transactions 거래·마무리

Payment Methods (결제 방법)

Korea is a highly cashless society — most places accept credit cards (신용카드) or debit cards (체크카드), and mobile payment via apps like KakaoPay and Samsung Pay is extremely common. Cash (현금) is still accepted everywhere but is used less often. When paying, you may be asked 카드요, 현금이요? (Card or cash?). Requesting a receipt is 영수증 주세요. Tax refund signs (Tax Refund) at stores mean you can claim VAT back at the airport as a tourist.

📱 카카오페이 & 삼성페이

KakaoPay (카카오페이) and Samsung Pay (삼성페이) are the dominant mobile payment apps in Korea. Tourists can use international credit cards almost everywhere. Look for the contactless payment symbol — Korea's adoption of contactless payment is among the highest in the world.
