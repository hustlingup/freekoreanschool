Getting Started

Korean Typing

Korea types on the Dubeolsik (두벌식) "2-set" layout: consonants on the left half of the keyboard, vowels on the right. Learn where each letter lives, then practise until the blocks build themselves.

Read the lesson, then practise at your own pace. All explanations and examples are on this page.

Meet the Keyboard 키보드 소개

The 2-Set Keyboard (두벌식) 두벌식 자판

Almost everyone in Korea types on the same layout: the 2-set keyboard, called 두벌식 (dubeolsik). It has been the national standard since 1985. "2-set" means the letters come in exactly two groups — consonants and vowels — and each group gets its own side of the keyboard. You never type a whole syllable block yourself. You type the letters one at a time, in the order you would say them, and the computer builds the block for you.

  • Consonants sit under your LEFT hand — ㅂ ㅈ ㄷ ㄱ ㅅ on the top row, ㅁ ㄴ ㅇ ㄹ ㅎ on the home row, ㅋ ㅌ ㅊ ㅍ on the bottom row.
  • Vowels sit under your RIGHT hand — ㅛ ㅕ ㅑ ㅐ ㅔ on the top row, ㅗ ㅓ ㅏ ㅣ on the home row, ㅠ ㅜ ㅡ on the bottom row.
  • Shift gives you the doubles — hold Shift and the five tense consonants ㅃ ㅉ ㄸ ㄲ ㅆ appear on ㅂ ㅈ ㄷ ㄱ ㅅ, plus the two wide vowels ㅒ ㅖ on ㅐ ㅔ.
  • That is the whole layout: 26 plain keys and 7 shifted ones. Learn 33 keys and you can write every Korean word there is.

Left hand, right hand

Because consonants are always on the left and vowels are always on the right, typing Korean turns into a steady left-right-left-right rhythm. Korean syllables are built the same way — consonant, then vowel — so your hands end up copying the shape of the language.

How Syllables Assemble 음절이 만들어지는 방법

Korean writing stacks letters into square blocks, but you do not have to draw those blocks. You just type the letters in order and they snap together on their own. Type ㅎ, then ㅏ, then ㄴ, and 한 appears one piece at a time: ㅎ → 하 → 한. Nothing else to press, no menu, no confirm key.

  • Type the letters in speaking order: opening consonant, then vowel, then the final consonant (받침) if the syllable has one.
  • A block closes by itself as soon as the next letter cannot fit into it. Type ㄱ ㅏ ㅁ ㅏ and you get 가마, not 감아 — the second ㅏ needs a consonant in front of it, so ㅁ jumps forward to start a new block.
  • This jump is called 받침 stealing, and it is exactly what Korean typists rely on. Type ㄱ ㅏ ㄱ ㅅ ㅏ and you get 각사: 가 → 각 → 갃 → 각사, with ㅅ pulled out of the double 받침 the moment the ㅏ arrives.
  • Backspace peels off one letter, not one block. From 각 it goes to 가, then to ㄱ, then to nothing.

Nothing to install

You do not need a Korean keyboard on your computer or phone to do this lesson. The keyboard on this page does the whole job — press the keys on your own keyboard, or tap the on-screen ones. Step 28 shows you how to add a real Korean keyboard to your devices for everywhere else.

Consonant Keys 자음 키

Keyboard keys

ㅁ ㄴㅁ and ㄴ — the left home row
Press ㅁ and then ㄴ three times each.
Rest your left hand on the home row: A S D F. ㅁ is on A under your little finger, ㄴ is on S under your ring finger. You never have to move your hand for these two.
ㅇ ㄹㅇ and ㄹ — still the home row
Press ㅇ and then ㄹ three times each.
Keep the same hand position. ㅇ is on D under your middle finger, ㄹ is on F under your index finger — F is the key with the little bump on it, so you can always find it without looking.
ㅎ ㄱㅎ and ㄱ — index-finger stretches
Press ㅎ and then ㄱ three times each.
Both are index-finger keys. ㅎ is on G — stay on the home row and reach one key to the right of F. ㄱ is on R, one row up from F. ㄱ is the single most common consonant in Korean, so it is worth getting comfortable.
ㅂ ㅈㅂ and ㅈ — top row, outside fingers
Press ㅂ and then ㅈ three times each.
Move your left hand up one row. ㅂ is on Q, straight above A, under your little finger. ㅈ is on W, straight above S, under your ring finger. Same fingers as ㅁ and ㄴ — just one row higher.
ㄷ ㅅㄷ and ㅅ — top row, inside fingers
Press ㄷ and then ㅅ three times each.
Still the top row. ㄷ is on E, above D, under your middle finger. ㅅ is on T — an index-finger stretch up and to the right, above G. ㅅ is very common as a 받침, so it will come up a lot.
ㅋ ㅌㅋ and ㅌ — bottom row, outside fingers
Press ㅋ and then ㅌ three times each.
Drop your left hand one row below home. ㅋ is on Z, under your little finger, and ㅌ is on X, under your ring finger. These are the breathy pair of ㄱ and ㄷ, and they sit in the least-used corner because Korean uses them less often.
ㅊ ㅍㅊ and ㅍ — bottom row, inside fingers
Press ㅊ and then ㅍ three times each.
Same bottom row. ㅊ is on C, under your middle finger, and ㅍ is on V, under your index finger. That is all 14 basic consonants: three rows, left hand only.

Vowel Keys 모음 키

Keyboard keys

KeyFinger and position
ㅗ ㅓㅗ and ㅓ — the right home row
Press ㅗ and then ㅓ three times each.
Now the right hand. Rest it on J K L and reach. ㅗ is on H, an index-finger stretch to the left. ㅓ is on J, the home key right under your index finger — J has a bump on it too.
ㅏ ㅣㅏ and ㅣ — no movement at all
Press ㅏ and then ㅣ three times each.
These two never move. ㅏ is on K under your middle finger and ㅣ is on L under your ring finger. ㅏ is the most common vowel in Korean, and it is already sitting under a resting finger.
ㅛ ㅕㅛ and ㅕ — top row, index finger
Press ㅛ and then ㅕ three times each.
Move your right hand up one row. Both are index-finger keys: ㅛ is on Y, a stretch up and to the left, and ㅕ is on U, straight above J. Notice the pattern — the y-vowels ㅛ ㅕ sit one row above their plain partners ㅗ ㅓ.
ㅑ ㅐㅑ and ㅐ — top row, middle and ring
Press ㅑ and then ㅐ three times each.
Still the top row. ㅑ is on I, straight above K, under your middle finger — again one row above its plain partner ㅏ. ㅐ is on O, under your ring finger.
ㅔㅔ — the far corner
Press ㅔ three times.
ㅔ is on P, the top-row key under your right little finger — the furthest vowel from home. ㅐ and ㅔ sound almost the same in modern Korean, and they sit side by side on O and P, so learn them as a pair.
ㅠ ㅜ ㅡㅠ, ㅜ and ㅡ — vowels on the bottom row
Press ㅠ, then ㅜ, then ㅡ, three times each.
Three keys in a row along the bottom: ㅠ on B, ㅜ on N, ㅡ on M. B is a stretch for your LEFT index finger, while N and M sit under your right index. ㅡ on M matters more than it looks — it is the vowel inside 은, 는, 를 and most other grammar endings.

Shift Keys 쌍자음 키

Keyboard keys

KeyFinger and position
ㅃ ㅉㅃ and ㅉ — hold Shift
Hold Shift and press ㅃ, then ㅉ, three times each.
The five tense consonants are the plain keys with Shift held down. ㅃ is Shift + Q (where ㅂ lives) and ㅉ is Shift + W (where ㅈ lives). Because both are left-hand keys, hold the Shift on the RIGHT with your right little finger while your left hand types — the keyboard highlights which Shift to use. On a touchscreen, tap ⇧ first — it stays down for exactly one key.
ㄸ ㄲ ㅆㄸ, ㄲ and ㅆ — the other three doubles
Hold Shift and press ㄸ, then ㄲ, then ㅆ, three times each.
Same rule, three more keys: ㄸ is Shift + E, ㄲ is Shift + R, ㅆ is Shift + T — all left-hand keys, so again hold the RIGHT Shift with your right little finger. These three turn up constantly — 진짜, 꼭, 있어요 — so the Shift reflex is worth drilling now.
ㅒ ㅖㅒ and ㅖ — shifted vowels
Hold Shift and press ㅒ, then ㅖ, three times each.
Shift works on the vowel side too, but only on two keys: ㅒ is Shift + O (above ㅐ) and ㅖ is Shift + P (above ㅔ). These are right-hand keys, so this time use the LEFT Shift with your left little finger — always the hand opposite the letter. Every other vowel you will ever need is either a plain key or two plain keys typed one after the other.

Type Syllables 음절 입력

Typing practice

Consonant Drill

A consonant appears — press its key. 20 letters in a row.

Go for accuracy, not speed. Slow and correct builds the habit; fast and wrong builds a habit you will have to unlearn. The speed comes on its own once your fingers stop hunting.

Vowel Drill

A vowel appears — press its key. 15 letters in a row.

Every one of these is under your right hand. If you find yourself looking down, try naming the finger before you press — index, middle, ring, little.

Simple Syllables

Type each syllable: one consonant, then one vowel. It completes on its own.

Two keystrokes each, and every one of them is left hand then right hand. There is no key to press to "finish" the block — the moment the vowel lands, the syllable is done and the drill moves on.

Syllables With a 받침

Type each syllable: consonant, vowel, then the final consonant underneath.

Three keystrokes each: left, right, left. Watch the block change shape as the third letter drops into place — 하 becomes 한 the instant you press ㄴ.

Combined Vowels

These vowels are two keys, typed one after the other.

ㅘ has no key of its own — you type ㅗ then ㅏ and the two merge. Same idea for ㅝ (ㅜ + ㅓ), ㅢ (ㅡ + ㅣ), ㅙ (ㅗ + ㅐ) and ㅟ (ㅜ + ㅣ). Only ㅐ ㅔ ㅒ ㅖ have their own keys.

Typing practice

One-Syllable Words

Type the word, then press Space to send it.

Word mode is different from the syllable drill: nothing advances until you press Space. That is your submit key. If you mistype, Backspace peels off one letter at a time so you can fix it before you send.

Two-Syllable Words

Type the whole word, then press Space.

Keep typing straight through the word — do not pause between the syllables. 한국 is six keystrokes in one run: ㅎ ㅏ ㄴ ㄱ ㅜ ㄱ. The blocks divide themselves.

Type the whole phrase, then press Space.

60-Second Sprint

Type as many words as you can in 60 seconds. Space sends each one.

Korean typing speed is counted in 타/분 — keystrokes per minute, not words per minute. An average Korean adult types somewhere around 300–400 타/분. If you are over 200 as a beginner, you are doing well; Korean professionals run past 600.

Type Korean on Your Own Devices 내 기기에 한국어 자판 추가하기

First, the honest part: you do not need to install anything to practise here. Every drill in this lesson works with no Korean keyboard on your device at all — the page reads which physical key you pressed, and on a phone you simply tap the keys on screen. But once you want to text a Korean friend, search in Korean, or fill in a form, you need a real Korean keyboard on your device. Here is how to add one. Everywhere below, pick the plain 2-set / 두벌식 layout — it is the same one you just learned.

  • iPhone / iPad — Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards → Add New Keyboard… → Korean. It then asks which layout: choose Standard (that is 두벌식; "10 Key" is the small phone-pad layout instead). Switch keyboards by holding the globe key at the bottom of the keyboard.
  • Android — easiest from the keyboard itself: open any app you can type in, tap the settings icon on the keyboard's top bar → Languages → Add keyboard → Korean → 2벌식 (2-set) → Done. Via the Settings app it is Settings → System → Keyboard → On-screen keyboard → Gboard → Languages → Add keyboard. To switch while typing, touch and hold the space bar.
  • Windows 11 — Settings → Time & language → Language & region → Add a language → search 한국어 (Korean) → Next → Install. Switch input with Win + Space. Once Korean is selected, the right Alt key toggles between Hangul and English — on a Korean keyboard that key is printed 한/영.
  • macOS — System Settings → Keyboard → Text Input → Edit… → click + → Korean → 2-Set Korean → Add. Switch input sources with Ctrl + Space; with Korean active, Caps Lock flips between Hangul and English.

Your practice transfers

Every one of these is the same 두벌식 layout you have been drilling: consonants left, vowels right, Shift for the doubles. Nothing to relearn — the muscle memory you built on this page works the moment you switch your phone or laptop over.
