Stage 1 · First & Second Person 1·2인칭

Korean Pronouns Overview 대명사 개요

Korean has two registers for first-person pronouns: polite and casual. 저 (jeo) is the polite 'I', used with strangers, seniors, and in formal settings. 나 (na) is the casual 'I', used with close friends and younger people. Crucially, Korean very often drops the subject pronoun entirely when context is clear — 어디 가요? can mean 'Where are you going?' without any pronoun at all. Mastering when NOT to use a pronoun is as important as knowing the pronouns themselves.

🔇 Pro-drop language

Korean is a pro-drop language — when the subject is obvious from context or was mentioned recently, drop the pronoun. Leaving it in can sound unnatural or even slightly rude (overly direct). 저 괜찮아요 and 괜찮아요 both mean 'I'm fine', but the shorter form is more natural.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
저jeoI / me (polite)
나naI / me (casual)
제가je-gaI (subject, polite) — 저 + subject marker 가 → 제가
너neoyou (casual) — rarely said to adults

Check your understanding

You are meeting your new boss for the first time. Which word for 'I' do you use?
Answer: 저 (나 · 저 · 내 · 제)

Saying 'You' in Korean '당신'과 호칭

Korean avoids the direct pronoun 'you' (당신) in most everyday speech — it can sound cold, confrontational, or overly formal depending on context. Instead, Koreans use the person's name, title, or relationship term. A teacher would be called 선생님 (teacher), not 당신. A friend would be called by name. The pronoun 당신 appears in songs, poetry, and formal writing but is uncommon in daily conversation.

💬 How to address people without 당신

Use the person's name + 씨 for adults you don't know well: 김민준씨. Use their job title: 사장님 (boss), 과장님 (manager). Use relationship terms: 언니, 오빠, 아저씨. These are far more natural than 당신.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
저는jeo-neunI (topic, polite) — 저 + topic marker 는 → 저는

Check your understanding

Why do Koreans rarely say 당신 in conversation?
Answer: It can sound cold or confrontational (It is grammatically incorrect · It can sound cold or confrontational · It is reserved for speaking to children · It is recent slang among young people)

Stage 2 · Third Person & We 3인칭·우리

Third Person & We (그·그녀·우리)

Korean rarely uses he/she pronouns in speech. Instead, Koreans say the person's name or use demonstratives: 그 사람 (that person), 이 사람 (this person). 그 (geu) and 그녀 (geunyeo) do exist — he/she — but they appear mostly in writing and translation. 우리 (uri) means 'we' or 'our'. Interestingly, Koreans use 우리 where English uses 'my': 우리 엄마 (my mom, literally 'our mom') — this reflects a collectivist cultural sense of shared family.

🏠 우리 = my (for family & country)

우리 나라 (our country), 우리 집 (my/our house), 우리 엄마 (my mom) — these are natural and common. Using 나의 나라 or 나의 집 instead sounds unnatural, almost cold.

Stage 3 · Demonstratives 지시대명사

Demonstrative Pronouns (이·그·저) 지시대명사 이·그·저

Korean has three levels of demonstratives based on spatial distance. 이 (i) = near the speaker. 그 (geu) = near the listener or previously mentioned. 저 (jeo) = far from both speaker and listener. These attach to 것 (geot, 'thing') to form pronouns: 이것 (this), 그것 (that), 저것 (that over there). In casual speech these are shortened: 이거, 그거, 저거. The same 이/그/저 roots also work with place words: 여기 (here), 거기 (there), 저기 (over there).

👆 이/그/저 vs 여기/거기/저기

이/그/저 + 것 = this/that thing. 이/그/저 + -(e)gi = here/there. So 여기 (yeo-gi) = here (near speaker), 거기 (geo-gi) = there (near listener), 저기 (jeo-gi) = over there (far from both).

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
이것i-geotthis (thing) — near speaker
그것geu-geotthat (thing) — near listener or already mentioned
저것jeo-geotthat thing over there — far from both
이거i-geothis one (casual form of 이것)

Check your understanding

Which demonstrative refers to something far from BOTH the speaker AND the listener?
Answer: 저것 (이것 · 그것 · 저것 · 어떤 것)

You are telling a friend about a man you mentioned a moment ago, and he is not in the room. In natural spoken Korean you say…

Stage 4 · Question Pronouns 의문대명사

Question Pronouns (의문대명사)

Korean question words are placed in the same sentence position as the word they replace — unlike English, which moves 'what' and 'where' to the front. Compare: 영화 봐요? (You watch a movie?) vs 뭐 봐요? (What do you watch?). The question word simply swaps into the original slot. The main question pronouns are: 누구 (who), 무엇/뭐 (what), 어디 (where), 언제 (when), 왜 (why), 어떻게 (how), 얼마 (how much).

❓ No question inversion needed

English moves the question word to the front and inverts subject-verb order: 'What are you doing?' Korean keeps the word order the same as a statement and just swaps in the question word: 뭐 해요? (What [are you] doing?) — literally 'what do?'

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
누구nu-guwho
무엇mu-eotwhat (formal)
뭐mwowhat (casual, very common)
어디eo-diwhere

Check your understanding

Which question pronoun means 'who'?
Answer: 누구 (뭐 · 어디 · 누구 · 왜)
