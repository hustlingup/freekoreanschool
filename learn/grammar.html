Stage 1 · Sentence 문장

Word Order — SOV 어순

Korean follows Subject → Object → Verb (SOV) order. The verb always comes LAST.

  • English (SVO): I eat rice.
  • Korean (SOV): 나는 밥을 먹어요. (I rice eat.)
  • Tip: Listen for the final word — that's the verb, the action!

💡 Grammar Tip

Once you know the verb comes last, everything clicks. The rest of the sentence can rearrange — Korean speakers use particles to keep things clear.

나는 밥을 먹어요na-neun bab-eul meog-eo-yoI eat rice. (Subject + Object + Verb)

Stage 2 · Particles 조사

Korean Particles 조사

Particles attach to nouns to show their role: topic, subject, object, location. They replace fixed word order.

  • 은/는 → Topic marker (은 after consonant, 는 after vowel)
  • 이/가 → Subject marker (이 after consonant, 가 after vowel)
  • 을/를 → Object marker (을 after consonant, 를 after vowel)
  • 에 → Location / Direction
  • 에서 → Location of action
  • 의 → Possessive ('s / of)

Stage 3 · Conjugation 활용

Verbs — Dictionary Form 동사 기본형

All Korean verbs in dictionary form end in 다 (da). Remove 다 to get the verb stem, then add an ending.

  • 가다 (to go) → stem: 가-
  • 먹다 (to eat) → stem: 먹-
  • 공부하다 (to study) → stem: 공부하-
  • 하다 (to do) → stem: 하-

Present Tense: -아요 / -어요 현재형

Add -아요 after ㅏ or ㅗ stems. Add -어요 for all others. 하다 verbs use -해요.

  • 가다 → 가요 (go · ㅏ stem)
  • 오다 → 와요 (come · ㅗ stem)
  • 먹다 → 먹어요 (eat · other)
  • 마시다 → 마셔요 (drink · other)
  • 공부하다 → 공부해요 (study · 하다)

💡 Vowel Rule

ㅏ and ㅗ are 'bright' vowels → -아요. All other vowels are 'dark' → -어요. Once you know the stem vowel, conjugation is automatic.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
가요ga-yoI go / Let's go. (가다 → 가요, polite present)
먹어요meog-eo-yoI eat / eating. (먹다 → 먹어요, polite present)

Answer: 해요 (해요 · 하요 · 하아요 · 했어요)

Past Tense: -았어요 / -었어요 과거형

Add -았어요 after ㅏ/ㅗ stems and -었어요 for others. 하다 → 했어요.

  • 가다 → 갔어요 (went)
  • 오다 → 왔어요 (came)
  • 먹다 → 먹었어요 (ate)
  • 마시다 → 마셨어요 (drank)
  • 공부하다 → 공부했어요 (studied)

Future Tense: -(으)ㄹ 거예요 미래형

Add -(으)ㄹ 거예요 to the verb stem to talk about future plans or predictions.

  • 가다 → 갈 거예요 (will go)
  • 먹다 → 먹을 거예요 (will eat)
  • 공부하다 → 공부할 거예요 (will study)

Check your understanding

가다 (to go) → future tense?
Answer: 갈 거예요 (갈 거예요 · 갔어요 · 가요 · 가고 있어요)

Stage 4 · Negation 부정문

Making Sentences Negative 부정문

Short negation: 안 + verb. Long negation: verb stem + 지 않아요. Cannot: 못 + verb.

  • 안 먹어요 (don't eat — short form)
  • 먹지 않아요 (don't eat — long form)
  • 못 가요 (can't go — unable to)

Check your understanding

'I don't eat' — short negation form
Answer: 안 먹어요 (안 먹어요 · 먹지 않아요 · 못 먹어요 · 안 먹었어요)

Stage 5 · Questions 의문문

Forming Questions 의문문

Korean questions use the SAME word order as statements — just add rising intonation (↑) or a question mark.

  • 뭐 / 무엇 — What
  • 누구 — Who
  • 어디 — Where
  • 언제 — When
  • 왜 — Why
  • 어떻게 — How
  • 얼마 — How much
  • 몇 — How many

💡 Grammar Tip

밥을 먹어요 = I eat rice. 밥을 먹어요? = Do you eat rice? Same words — just a rising tone at the end. No word inversion like English!

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
이름이 뭐예요?i-reum-i mwo-ye-yo?What is your name?

Check your understanding

'Where' in Korean?
Answer: 어디 (어디 · 뭐 · 왜 · 언제)

Stage 6 · Patterns 문형

Essential Sentence Patterns 핵심 문형

Master these 6 patterns to express the most common ideas in Korean conversations.

  • ~이에요/예요 — is/am/are (noun): 학생이에요 (I'm a student)
  • ~고 싶어요 — want to: 한국에 가고 싶어요 (I want to go to Korea)
  • ~ㄹ/을 수 있어요 — can: 한국어를 할 수 있어요 (I can speak Korean)
  • ~아/어야 해요 — must: 공부해야 해요 (I must study)
  • ~(으)면 — if/when: 비가 오면 집에 있어요 (If it rains, I stay home)
  • ~때문에 — because of: 비 때문에 못 가요 (Can't go because of rain)

Check your understanding

Pattern for 'I want to go to Korea': 한국에 ___
Answer: 가고 싶어요 (가고 싶어요 · 가야 해요 · 갈 거예요 · 갈 수 있어요)

Stage 7 · Connectors 접속사

Connectors 접속사

These 4 conjunctions connect sentences. Place them at the START of the second sentence.

  • 그리고 — And / And then (adds information or sequence)
  • 그래서 — So / Therefore (cause → result)
  • 그렇지만 — But / However (strong contrast, formal)
  • 그런데 — But / By the way (soft contrast, casual — most common!)

💡 Usage Tip

그런데 is one of the most common words in spoken Korean — it softens contrast and shifts topics smoothly. 그렇지만 is stronger and more formal.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
비가 왔어요. 그래서 집에 있었어요.bi-ga wass-eo-yo. geu-rae-seo jib-e iss-eoss-eo-yo.It rained. So I stayed home.

Check your understanding

Soft contrast or topic shift — most common in spoken Korean?
Answer: 그런데 (그런데 · 그렇지만 · 그래서 · 그리고)

Stage 8 · And/With 와/랑

And, With -하고, -(이)랑, 와/과

Use these particles between nouns (not sentences) to mean 'and' or 'with'.

  • -하고 — after any noun, neutral/casual: 친구하고 갔어요 (went with a friend)
  • -(이)랑 — 이랑 (consonant) / 랑 (vowel), very casual: 오빠랑 놀았어요 (played with older brother)
  • -와/과 — 와 (vowel) / 과 (consonant), formal/written: 선생님과 상담했어요 (consulted with teacher)

Stage 9 · To/From 한테

To/From Someone -한테, -한테서

Use person-directional particles when giving to or receiving from people, not places.

  • -한테 — to (a person): 친구한테 전화했어요 (called my friend)
  • -한테서 — from (a person): 선생님한테서 배웠어요 (learned from teacher)
  • -에게 / -에게서 — formal equivalents
  • -께 — to (honorific, for elders): 선생님께 드렸어요

Stage 10 · Time 시간

Telling Time 시간 말하기

Use NATIVE Korean numbers for hours (시) and SINO-KOREAN numbers for minutes (분). AM = 오전, PM = 오후.

  • Hours (시): 한(1), 두(2), 세(3), 네(4), 다섯(5)... + 시
  • Minutes (분): 일(1), 이(2), 삼(3), 사(4), 오(5)... + 분
  • Half past: 반 — 세 시 반 = 3:30
  • Example: 오후 두 시 삼십 분 = 2:30 PM

💡 Key Pattern

Hours use Native Korean numbers (한, 두, 세...). Minutes use Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼...). Half past = 반. AM = 오전, PM = 오후.

Stage 11 · Counters 단위

Counters 단위 명사

Korean uses specific counters after [Noun] + [Number]. Native Korean numbers (한, 두, 세...) are used with most counters.

  • 개 — general objects: 사과 세 개 (3 apples)
  • 명 — people (neutral): 학생 두 명 (2 students)
  • 분 — people (honorific): 손님 두 분 (2 guests)
  • 마리 — animals: 고양이 한 마리 (1 cat)
  • 권 — books: 책 세 권 (3 books)
  • 잔 — cups/drinks: 커피 두 잔 (2 coffees)
  • 번 — times/turns: 세 번 (3 times)

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
사과 세 개 주세요sa-gwa se gae ju-se-yoPlease give me three apples.

Check your understanding

Counter for people (neutral)?
Answer: 명 (명 · 분 · 마리 · 개)

Stage 12 · Progressive 진행형

Present Progressive -고 있어요

Add -고 있어요 to the verb stem to say someone IS CURRENTLY doing something (the Korean '-ing' form).

  • 먹다 → 먹고 있어요 (is eating)
  • 가다 → 가고 있어요 (is going)
  • 공부하다 → 공부하고 있어요 (is studying)
  • 읽다 → 읽고 있어요 (is reading)

💡 Progressive vs Simple

먹어요 = I eat (general or right now, context-dependent). 먹고 있어요 = I am eating (specifically in progress at this moment). The progressive adds 'currently in action.'

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
지금 밥을 먹고 있어요ji-geum bab-eul meok-go iss-eo-yoI am eating right now.

Check your understanding

'is studying' → 공부하다 + -고 있어요 =
Answer: 공부하고 있어요 (공부하고 있어요 · 공부고 있어요 · 공부하고 있다 · 공부해요)

Stage 13 · Self-Intro 자기소개

Self Introduction 자기소개

Key vocabulary: 이름 (name), 나이 (age), 나라 (country), 직업 (job), 취미 (hobby), 고향 (hometown).

  • 안녕하세요! 저는 [name]이에요/예요.
  • 저는 [나라]에서 왔어요. (I'm from [country].)
  • 저는 [직업]이에요. (I'm a [job].)
  • 제 취미는 [hobby]예요. (My hobby is [hobby].)
  • 잘 부탁드려요! (Nice to meet you!)

💡 Template

Use 안녕하세요 + 잘 부탁드려요 for formal settings. With friends or peers: 안녕! + 잘 부탁해! Always bow slightly when introducing yourself in person.

Stage 14 · Dates 날짜

Dates and Months 날짜

Korean dates use Sino-Korean numbers in Year → Month → Day order. June = 유월, October = 시월 (exceptions).

  • 일월(1월), 이월(2월), 삼월(3월), 사월(4월), 오월(5월), 유월(6월)
  • 칠월(7월), 팔월(8월), 구월(9월), 시월(10월), 십일월(11월), 십이월(12월)
  • Date format: 2026년 6월 16일 (June 16, 2026)
  • 오늘이 며칠이에요? — What is today's date?

Stage 15 · Adverbs 부사

Degree Adverbs 정도 부사

Degree adverbs go directly before the word they modify: 조금 (a little), 정말 (really), 아주 (very), 많이 (a lot).

  • 조금 / 좀 — a little (좀 is softer/casual)
  • 정말 — really / truly (neutral)
  • 진짜 — really (casual, stronger feel)
  • 아주 — very
  • 많이 — a lot / much
  • 별로 + negative — not really (별로 안 좋아요 = I don't really like it)
  • 전혀 + negative — not at all (전혀 모르겠어요 = I have no idea at all)

💡 Negative Adverbs

별로 and 전혀 MUST be used with a negative verb (안, 못, 없다, 모르다). Saying 별로 좋아요 (without negation) is ungrammatical. Think of them as 'not really' and 'not at all'.

KoreanPronunciationMeaning

Stage 16 · Nominalizer 명사화

Nominalizer: -는 것 -는 것

Adding -는 것 to a verb stem creates a noun phrase — 'the act of doing ~'. It makes verbs act like nouns.

  • 먹는 것 — the act of eating
  • 배우는 것 — the act of learning
  • 한국어를 배우는 것이 재미있어요 — Learning Korean is interesting
  • 요리하는 것을 좋아해요 — I like cooking (the act of cooking)

💡 Tense Forms

-는 것 (present/habitual) · -(으)ㄴ 것 (past/completed) · -(으)ㄹ 것 (future/planned). The present form is the most common in daily speech.

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
한국어를 배우는 것이 재미있어요han-gug-eo-reul bae-u-neun geot-i jae-mi-iss-eo-yoLearning Korean is interesting.

Check your understanding

'Learning Korean is interesting' — 한국어를 ___ 재미있어요
Answer: 배우는 것이 (배우는 것이 · 배워요 · 배우고 · 배웠어요)

Stage 17 · Comparatives 비교

Comparatives 비교 표현

Structure: [A]이/가 [B]보다 더 [adjective]. 보다 means 'than' and 더 means 'more'.

  • 한국어가 영어보다 더 어려워요 — Korean is harder than English
  • 오늘이 어제보다 더 더워요 — Today is hotter than yesterday
  • 더 can be dropped in casual speech: 한국어가 영어보다 어려워요

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
한국어가 일본어보다 어려워요han-gug-eo-ga il-bon-eo-bo-da eo-ryeo-wo-yoKorean is harder than Japanese.

Check your understanding

How to say 'more' in a Korean comparison?
Answer: 더 (더 · 많이 · 아주 · 보다)

Stage 18 · Like 좋다/좋아하다

좋다 vs 좋아하다

좋다 uses subject particle (이/가): 한국어가 좋아요. 좋아하다 uses object particle (을/를): 한국어를 좋아해요.

  • 좋다 — to be good / feel good (state): 커피가 좋아요 (I like coffee / Coffee is good)
  • 좋아하다 — to like (active preference): 커피를 좋아해요 (I like coffee)
  • Both translate as 'I like' but 좋다 focuses on the feeling, 좋아하다 on the preference

💡 Key Difference

좋아요 → subject particle (이/가) precedes it. 좋아해요 → object particle (을/를) precedes it. When in doubt, 좋아해요 sounds more natural for expressing preferences.

Check your understanding

'I like coffee' using 좋아하다 — 커피___ 좋아해요
Answer: 를 (를 · 가 · 에 · 도)

Stage 19 · Still/Already 아직/이미

Still & Already 아직 · 이미 · 벌써

아직 + negative verb = still not. 벌써 = already (sooner than expected). 이미 = already (neutral, formal).

  • 아직 안 먹었어요 — Haven't eaten yet (still not)
  • 아직 여기 있어요 — Still here (ongoing)
  • 벌써 도착했어요? — Already arrived? (surprised)
  • 이미 알아요 — I already know (neutral)

Check your understanding

'아직' means?
Answer: Still / Not yet (Still / Not yet · Already · Even · More)

Stage 20 · Indefinite 막연

Someone, Something 막연한 대상

Combine question words with context to express indefinite ideas like 'someone' or 'nothing'.

  • 누군가 — someone: 누군가 왔어요 (Someone came)
  • 무언가 / 뭔가 — something: 뭔가 이상해요 (Something is strange)
  • 어딘가 — somewhere: 어딘가에 있어요 (It's somewhere)
  • 아무도 + negative — no one: 아무도 없어요 (No one is here)
  • 아무것도 + negative — nothing: 아무것도 몰라요 (I know nothing)

Check your understanding

'No one is here' — 아무도 ___
Answer: 없어요 (없어요 · 있어요 · 알아요 · 와요)

Stage 21 · Imperative 명령형

Imperative: -(으)세요 명령형

Add -(으)세요 to a verb stem to make a polite request or command.

  • 가다 → 가세요 (please go)
  • 앉다 → 앉으세요 (please sit)
  • 먹다 → 드세요 (please eat — honorific)

Check your understanding

앉다 (to sit) → polite request form?
Answer: 앉으세요 (앉으세요 · 앉세요 · 앉아세요 · 앉지 마세요)

Stage 22 · Don't 금지형

Don't: -지 마세요 금지형

Add -지 마세요 to a verb stem to politely tell someone NOT to do something.

  • 말하다 → 말하지 마세요 (please don't speak)
  • 가다 → 가지 마세요 (please don't go)
  • 먹다 → 먹지 마세요 (please don't eat)

Words and phrases

KoreanPronunciationMeaning
천천히 말해 주세요cheon-cheon-hi mal-hae ju-se-yoPlease speak slowly.

Check your understanding

'Please don't speak' in Korean?
Answer: 말하지 마세요 (말하지 마세요 · 말해 주세요 · 말하세요 · 말 안 해요)

Stage 23 · Method 방법

Method: -(으)로 방법

-(으)로 marks method or means — 'by' or 'with' a tool/way of doing something.

  • -으로 after a consonant, -로 after a vowel or ㄹ: 버스로 가요 (go by bus)
  • 지하철로 와요 (come by subway)
  • 한국어로 말해요 (speak in Korean)

Check your understanding

'I go by bus' — 버스___ 가요
Answer: 로 (로 · 으로 · 에서 · 를)

Stage 24 · Good/Poor 잘함/못함

Good/Poor At 잘하다 · 못하다

잘하다 = good at. 못하다 = poor at. Both attach after the object particle 을/를.

  • 한국어를 잘해요 (good at Korean)
  • 수학을 못해요 (bad at math)
  • 수영을 잘 못해요 (not very good at swimming)

Check your understanding

'good at Korean' — 한국어를 ___
Answer: 잘해요 (잘해요 · 못해요 · 좋아해요 · 잘 못해요)

Stage 25 · All/More 다/더

All, More: 다, 더 다 · 더

다 = all/everything. 더 = more. Both are simple adverbs placed before the verb.

  • 다 먹었어요 — ate everything / all of it
  • 더 주세요 — please give (me) more
  • 다 and 더 are used very differently depending on context

Check your understanding

'I ate it all' — ___ 먹었어요
Answer: 다 (다 · 더 · 좀 · 잘)

Stage 26 · -도 Advanced 도

All, More & -도 -도 응용

-도 has 4 advanced uses beyond simple 'also': emphasis, emphatic negation, and 'both...and'.

  • 아이도 알아요 — even children know (emphasis: unexpected inclusion)
  • 하나도 없어요 — not even one (emphatic negation: 하나도 + negative)
  • 먹기도 해요 — sometimes eats / also eats (-기도 하다)
  • 좋기도 하고 나쁘기도 해요 — both good and bad
