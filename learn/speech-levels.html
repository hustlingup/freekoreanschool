Stage 1 · 합쇼체 (Formal) 합쇼체

Korean Speech Levels Overview 높임말 개요

Korean has a system of speech levels (높임말) that determines how formal or polite your language is. The level you choose depends on the social relationship, age, rank, and context of the conversation. The three levels you'll encounter most are: 합쇼체 (formal polite, used in presentations, military, and customer service), 해요체 (everyday polite, the default for adults talking to non-close acquaintances), and 반말 (casual speech, used with close friends and younger people). Getting the level wrong can come across as rude, cold, or over-familiar.

🇰🇷 Why speech levels matter in Korea

Korea is a hierarchical society shaped by Confucian values — age, rank, and relationship all determine how you speak. Using 반말 (casual speech) with a stranger or senior is rude. Using over-formal 합쇼체 with a friend can feel stiff and distant. Matching the right level is a key social skill.

합쇼체 — The Formal Register 합쇼체

합쇼체 is the highest formal speech level in Korean. It is used in TV news broadcasts, military orders, formal presentations, hotel/airline staff speech, and customer service. Verb endings change to -ㅂ니다 / -습니다 for statements and -ㅂ니까? / -습니까? for questions. The vocabulary also shifts — 밥 (food) becomes 식사, 있어요 becomes 있습니다. This level signals maximum respect and professionalism.

📺 Where to hear 합쇼체

Turn on Korean news (KBS, MBC, SBS) and you'll hear 합쇼체 throughout. Hotel staff, airline announcements, formal ceremonies, and military settings all use this level. It's also the level used in written formal documents, though pure written style (문어체) differs slightly.

합쇼체 Verb Endings (-ㅂ니다/-습니다) -ㅂ니다/-습니다

The hallmark of 합쇼체 is the -ㅂ니다 / -습니다 ending. Use -ㅂ니다 after verb stems ending in a vowel: 가다 → 갑니다 (I go). Use -습니다 after stems ending in a consonant: 먹다 → 먹습니다 (I eat). For questions, the ending changes to -ㅂ니까? / -습니까?: 가다 → 갑니까? (Are you going?). The pronunciation note: 갑니다 is pronounced [감니다] due to nasal assimilation.

🔤 Pronunciation shift in -ㅂ니다

갑니다 (I go) is spelled 갑니다 but pronounced 감니다. This is because ㅂ before ㄴ changes to ㅁ by nasal assimilation (same mouth position, nasal airflow). This pattern appears throughout 합쇼체 forms.

Stage 2 · 해요체 (Polite) 해요체

해요체 — Everyday Polite Speech 해요체

해요체 is the speech level you will use most in daily Korean life. It is polite enough for strangers and acquaintances but warm enough to feel natural. The ending -아요 / -어요 / -해요 is added to the verb stem. 해요체 is used in shops, with coworkers you're not extremely close to, with adults you're meeting for the first time, and in most learner-to-native interactions. It is the 'default polite' of Korean.

😊 해요체 is your default setting

When in doubt, use 해요체. It's the safest, most universally appropriate level in Korea. You can never go wrong being politely respectful. Switching to 합쇼체 or 반말 should only happen when the situation clearly calls for it.

-아요 / -어요 Pattern -아요/-어요 패턴

The 해요체 ending rule: add -아요 if the last vowel of the stem is ㅏ or ㅗ (bright vowels), and add -어요 for all other vowels. 하다 verbs use -해요 instead. Examples: 가다 → 가 + 아요 = 가요 (contracted). 먹다 → 먹 + 어요 = 먹어요. 공부하다 → 공부해요. In practice, 아/어 often contract with the stem's final vowel: 가 + 아요 → 가요, 오 + 아요 → 와요.

📋 Quick chart

가다 → 가요. 오다 → 와요. 먹다 → 먹어요. 마시다 → 마셔요. 하다 → 해요. 보다 → 봐요. 자다 → 자요. The endings follow a vowel harmony pattern — try to recognize the bright/dark vowel distinction in the stem.

Stage 3 · 반말 (Casual) 반말

반말 — Casual Speech 반말

반말 (ban-mal, literally 'half-speech') is the casual register used among close friends of similar age, with younger people, and within family. It drops the -요 from 해요체 endings: 가요 → 가, 먹어요 → 먹어, 좋아요 → 좋아. 반말 can sound rude if used with someone older or with a stranger — always start with 해요체 and switch to 반말 only if the other person initiates or explicitly suggests it. In K-dramas, characters switching from 해요체 to 반말 signals a big shift in closeness.

🤝 Getting permission to use 반말

A natural way to ask permission: '말 놓아도 돼요?' (Can I speak casually?) or '반말해도 돼요?' (Is it okay to use 반말?). You can also wait for the other person to naturally switch first — that's your invitation.

When to Use 반말 (and When NOT to) 반말 사용 규칙

Use 반말 with: close friends of similar age, younger siblings, younger students, or children. Do NOT use 반말 with: strangers, anyone older than you, your boss or teacher, service staff, or people you've just met (even if they seem similar in age). The mistake many Korean learners make is assuming that because someone is friendly, 반말 is appropriate. Friendliness and speech-level permission are separate — always wait for a signal.

⚠️ One exception: self-talk

When Koreans talk to themselves, mutter, or write diary entries, they use 반말 or even a neutral style without any endings. This is natural and not directed at anyone — so no politeness is needed. This is also why drama monologues and internal thoughts are in 반말.

Check your understanding

You're talking to a stranger your same age on the street. Which level should you use?
Answer: 해요체 (반말 · 해요체 · both are fine · 합쇼체 only)

Stage 4 · Choosing a Level 레벨 선택

문어체 — Written/Formal Style 문어체

문어체 (mun-eo-che, 'written language style') is used in formal writing — academic papers, news articles, legal documents, and literature. The verb ending is -다 (the plain dictionary form): 가다, 먹는다, 했다. It sounds unnatural in spoken conversation but appears everywhere in written Korean. Learners encounter this when reading Korean text and may wonder why it differs from what they've been taught — that's because textbooks teach spoken registers, while reading requires recognizing 문어체.

📝 문어체 vs 합쇼체

Both are formal, but 합쇼체 is for speech (speaker to listener) and 문어체 is for writing (no specific listener). A news anchor speaks in 합쇼체. A newspaper article is in 문어체. In K-dramas, you'll hear 문어체 when characters read aloud from books or letters.

Speech Level Comparison Chart 높임말 비교표

Here is how the same idea looks across speech levels. 'I eat': 먹습니다 (formal 합쇼체) → 먹어요 (polite 해요체) → 먹어 (casual 반말) → 먹는다 (written 문어체). 'Thank you': 감사합니다 (formal) → 감사해요 (polite) → 고마워 (casual). 'I go': 갑니다 (formal) → 가요 (polite) → 가 (casual) → 간다 (written). Noticing these patterns will help you identify which level a speaker is using when you watch Korean dramas or listen to native speech.

🎬 Use K-dramas to train your ear

K-dramas are a gold mine for speech level practice. Bosses speak in 합쇼체 to their team. Friends switch to 반말 with each other. Scenes in hospitals or offices use 해요체. If you notice a sudden change in speech level between characters — that's a dramatic moment.

Answer: 합쇼체 (반말 · 해요체 · 합쇼체 · 문어체)

Mixing Speech Levels — A Common Mistake 높임말 혼용 주의

One of the most common learner mistakes is mixing speech levels — using 먹어요 (해요체) in one sentence and 먹습니다 (합쇼체) in the next. Native speakers notice this immediately. It sounds like mixing formal and casual in the same breath. Choose one level for the conversation and stick to it. The only exception is intentional switching for rhetorical effect (like a business email that opens formally and ends warmly), but even this follows clear patterns.

❌ Don't mix endings mid-sentence

Wrong: 저는 학생이에요. 공부합니다. (Mixed 해요체 and 합쇼체). Correct: 저는 학생이에요. 공부해요. (All 해요체). Or: 저는 학생입니다. 공부합니다. (All 합쇼체). Consistency within a conversation signals fluency and social awareness.
