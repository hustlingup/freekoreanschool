Korean numbers you can actually use

The difficult part of Korean numbers is often choosing a system, rather than remembering a digit. A café gives you a useful contrast: two cups is 두 잔, while two thousand won is 이천 원. Learn each number together with what it counts.

Native Korean numbers are common for counting items and people, and for clock hours. Sino-Korean numbers are used for prices in won, minutes, dates, and phone-number digits. This is a beginner guide to common combinations, not a rule that every possible counter uses the same system. Learn unfamiliar measurement units with their actual number pattern.

Name the kind of thing you are counting

A counter follows the number and identifies the unit. A practical pattern is noun + number + counter: 커피 두 잔, “two cups of coffee.” Before a counter, 하나, 둘, 셋, and 넷 become 한, 두, 세, and 네. 스물 becomes 스무 in combinations such as 스무 살. These shorter forms belong with the following counter; do not shorten a standalone answer such as 하나, 둘, 셋 when simply counting aloud.

Notice that 분 has two different jobs in common number expressions. 손님 두 분 counts people respectfully using a native number. 이 분 means two minutes using a Sino-Korean number. A counter’s written form alone does not settle the meaning; read the situation.

Build a price in blocks

For prices, use 십 (ten), 백 (hundred), 천 (thousand), and 만 (ten thousand). 삼천 원 is 3,000 won; 오천오백 원 is 5,500 won. Korean groups large numbers around 만, so 12,000 is 만 이천, not a literal word-for-word translation of “twelve thousand.”

These are number-reading examples, not current café prices. On a real menu, confirm what the displayed amount includes. To ask a price, use 얼마예요? (“How much is it?”). You can point to an item and say 이거 얼마예요? (“How much is this?”).

One clock reading uses both systems

세 시 십오 분 is 3:15. 세 is the native short form for the hour, while 십오 is the Sino-Korean number for the minutes. At 2:30, say 두 시 삼십 분 or 두 시 반 (“half past two”). If the time could be ambiguous, add 오전 for a.m. or 오후 for p.m.

Practise hours separately from minutes before combining them. Write 1:10, say 한 시 first, then add 십 분. This reduces the temptation to use 일 시 simply because the minute part uses Sino-Korean numbers.

Check quantities, prices, and time

1. Order two cups of coffee.

Answer and explanation

커피 두 잔 주세요. 둘 shortens to 두 before the counter 잔.

2. Read 3,000 won.

Answer and explanation

삼천 원 Prices use Sino-Korean numbers; 삼 is three and 천 is thousand.

3. Which expression says 2:15?

Answer and explanation

두 시 십오 분 Clock hours use native Korean numbers and minutes use Sino-Korean numbers.

Your own number drill

Write three quantities, two sample prices, and one time. Cover the Korean and read them back from the digits. A useful answer set is 사과 한 개, 물 세 병, 책 두 권, 이천 원, 만 원, and 네 시 오 분. Check both the number system and any short form, rather than marking only the digit as correct.

Reference and next step

Use National Institute of Korean Language: Korean–English Learners’ Dictionary to hear 하나, 둘, 셋, 잔, 명, 분, 원 and compare their senses. The prices and situations above are original practice examples. Continue with number vocabulary and shopping, then use the café guide to put a quantity inside a request.
