Understand your first Korean restaurant visit

A restaurant visit combines language with small decisions: where to get water, how to ask for help, and what the dishes on the table are for. This guide gives you a flexible way to observe, ask, and respond. Its dialogue is a constructed learning example; it does not describe a restaurant we visited.

Read the setting before choosing a phrase

Imagine entering a small restaurant with a friend. You see a water dispenser, a menu, and small dishes already on the table. Start by looking for instructions rather than assuming every restaurant works the same way. 셀프 on a sign usually means that you serve yourself for the item named there. 물은 셀프입니다 means “Water is self-service.” It does not mean you must collect your entire meal.

The Korea Tourism Organization describes both self-service arrangements and places where staff help diners in its restaurant introduction. Use that as background, then follow the instructions at the actual restaurant. A bell may be provided to call staff; its presence is a useful cue, not a rule that every table has one.

Give the food and the words different jobs

WordMeaning hereA useful distinction
밥Cooked rice; also a meal in many expressions밥 먹었어요? asks whether someone has eaten, not necessarily whether they ate rice.
반찬Side dishes accompanying a mealThe small dishes are not necessarily separate main orders for each person.
국 / 찌개Soup / stewPresentation and portions vary; check what you ordered rather than identifying the arrangement from its English name alone.
추가An addition or extra orderAsk whether an extra item costs money rather than assuming that every extra is free.

The official food introduction explains 반찬 and the arrangement of a Korean meal. Our learning task is to use those distinctions: point to your rice, the shared items, and the main dish, then name each. If sharing is unclear, ask your companion or staff. Do not infer someone’s preferences from their nationality.

Ask for one thing clearly

RoleKoreanMeaning
Guest저기요. 물 좀 주세요.Excuse me. Please give us some water.
Staff물은 셀프예요.Water is self-service.
Guest아, 네. 감사합니다.Oh, okay. Thank you.
Guest, later반찬을 더 받을 수 있어요?Could we have more side dishes?

주세요 makes a request. 좀 literally relates to a small amount, but in a request like 물 좀 주세요 it can also soften the wording. It is not a substitute for a polite ending. 더 means “more,” so 반찬을 더 받을 수 있어요? asks about an additional serving.

Now replace 물 with 냅킨 (“napkin”). Then replace the direct request with 물은 어디에 있어요? (“Where is the water?”). The second question fits a self-service setting better because you are asking for the location instead of asking staff to bring it. Both are learning patterns, not a guarantee of the reply you will hear.

Handle a reply you did not expect

If you miss an instruction, say 다시 말해 주세요. (“Please say it again.”). If the person points somewhere, you can confirm 저기예요? (“Is it over there?”). A brief clarification is more useful than guessing which counter or machine they mean.

For an extra order, 추가하면 얼마예요? means “How much is it if we add that?” Point to the item or name it first. This avoids assuming that a menu item and a side-dish refill follow the same pricing rule. Prices, refill policies, and payment procedures belong to the particular business.

Before finishing, 계산은 어디에서 해요? asks where to pay. After a meal, 잘 먹었습니다. expresses appreciation for the meal. It describes a completed meal; compare 잘 먹겠습니다. before eating. Neither phrase requires pretending you enjoyed something you did not want to eat.

Choose a response for the setting

1. The sign says 물은 셀프입니다. What does it tell you?

Answer and explanation

You collect your own water. 물 names the item covered by the instruction. Do not extend that instruction to the whole meal.

2. You understand that water is self-service. Which question fits?

Answer and explanation

물은 어디에 있어요? Ask where the water is. 잘 먹었습니다 expresses appreciation after eating.

3. You want an additional menu item, but do not know its price. What helps?

Answer and explanation

Ask 추가하면 얼마예요? An extra order can differ from a refill. Ask about this item before agreeing.

4. You have finished the meal. Which expression fits the time?

Answer and explanation

잘 먹었습니다. 먹었습니다 refers to a completed action. 먹겠습니다 is the conventional before-meal expression.

Make it yours

Write a three-line scene: ask where a self-service item is, confirm the location, and thank the staff. Tomorrow, change the item without looking at the model. Check whether you are asking for a thing or asking where that thing is.

What the references support

Korea Tourism Organization: About Korean Food supports the background on side dishes, meal arrangements, and differing restaurant service systems. Accessed 2 October 2026. It is not a source for our constructed conversation and does not establish the policy of a specific restaurant.

Use National Institute of Korean Language: Korean–English Learners’ Dictionary to look up 반찬, 셀프, 추가, 계산, 더. These are lookup resources for meanings and pronunciation, not a claim that a dictionary has endorsed this guide. Continue with café ordering for quantities and the food overview for more dishes.
