YOUR PATH · 배움의 길

From your first letter to your first conversation

Korean rewards a specific order. Skip a step and later ones feel impossible; follow it and each one makes the next easier. This is the exact sequence Korean School is built around — and every stage below is a free lesson you can start today.

1. Read 한글 first — it takes hours, not months

Most learners are surprised that the alphabet is the easy part. 한글 (Hangul) was designed in 1443 to be learned quickly, and it shows: 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels, each with a shape that hints at how you say it. Once you can read 한글 — usually within a day or two of focused practice — Korean stops being a wall of symbols and becomes a language you can sound out. Nothing else you do here depends on memorising the alphabet the slow way, so we put it first and make it stick.

2. Build syllable blocks

Korean letters are not written in a line like English; they stack into square syllable blocks, so ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ becomes 한. Understanding this one rule turns two dozen letters into thousands of readable words. Our syllable-block lessons include an interactive builder that composes real Hangul as you choose each part, so the pattern becomes automatic instead of theoretical.

3. Fix pronunciation early

Korean sounds are consistent, which is good news, but a few — the tense consonants, the difference between ㅓ and ㅗ, and the way final consonants change before the next word — trip up nearly everyone. We cover these while your habits are still forming, with listen-and-repeat audio on every example, because unlearning a mispronunciation later is far harder than learning it right the first time.

4. Learn grammar as patterns, not rules

Korean grammar is genuinely different from English: the verb comes last (Subject–Object–Verb), particles mark who does what, and the ending of a verb carries politeness, tense, and mood all at once. That sounds daunting and is actually freeing — word order is flexible because the particles do the work. Our grammar track teaches the handful of high-frequency patterns that unlock everyday speech, with real example sentences rather than tables to memorise.

5. Grow vocabulary in context

With reading and grammar in place, vocabulary is where fluency actually accumulates. We organise more than 5,000 words into themed sets — food, travel, emotions, business, shopping — each with audio, example sentences, and flashcards that track what you have learned. Learning words inside a topic you care about is the difference between remembering ten and remembering a hundred.

6. Start speaking through culture

The last step is the one most courses never reach: using the language for something you enjoy. This is where the K-Culture guides come in. A K-Pop lyric, a drama scene, a food order — each is a low-pressure, high-motivation way to hear real Korean at natural speed and try producing your own. You do not wait until you are "ready" to engage with Korean culture; engaging with it is how you get ready.

WHY KOREAN · 왜 한국어인가

Why learn Korean in 2026?

Korean has gone from a niche language to one of the most-studied in the world in barely a decade, and the reasons are practical as well as cultural.

The culture you already follow is in Korean. The Korean Wave — 한류 (Hallyu) — put K-Pop, K-Drama, and Korean film onto phones and screens everywhere. Understanding even a little Korean transforms that from something you watch with subtitles into something you actually follow: the joke that does not translate, the honorific that signals respect, the lyric that means more than the caption said.

It makes travel richer. Korea is welcoming to visitors, but a traveller who can read 한글, order food, and ask for directions has a completely different trip — one that reaches the neighbourhood restaurant, the local market, and the conversation that no guidebook prints.

It opens work and study. Korea is a global centre for technology, manufacturing, beauty, and entertainment. Korean-language ability is a real advantage for business, and the TOPIK proficiency exam is the gateway to university study and many jobs in Korea. Our advanced tracks — business Korean, essay writing, and classical Korean — are built with those goals in mind.

The writing system is a joy to learn. There is a reason linguists praise 한글: it is one of the most logical scripts ever created, and being able to read it after an afternoon is a genuinely motivating first win. Few languages give back so much, so fast, for so little.

GETTING STARTED · 자주 묻는 질문

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to learn 한글?

Most people can read the Korean alphabet in a single focused afternoon and feel comfortable within a week of light practice. 한글 is phonetic and consistent, so once you know the letters you can sound out almost any word — long before you understand what it means. That early win is exactly why we start here.

Is Korean hard for English speakers?

It is different rather than simply hard. The grammar (verb-final word order, particles, speech levels) takes adjustment, but the alphabet is easy, the pronunciation is regular, and there are no tones to worry about as in Chinese or Vietnamese. Learners who follow a sensible order — alphabet, then sound, then patterns — usually find it far more approachable than its reputation suggests.

Do I need to learn Hanja (Chinese characters)?

No. Modern Korean is written almost entirely in 한글, and you can reach a high level of reading, speaking, and comprehension without studying Hanja at all. A little Hanja awareness can help you see how words are built later on, but it is optional and never a starting requirement.

Is Korean School really free?

Yes — every lesson, guide, quiz, and tool on this site is free to use, with no account required and no paywall. The site is supported by unobtrusive advertising, not subscriptions, so the content stays open to learners everywhere.

How is this different from a language app?

Apps are good at drilling isolated words; they rarely explain why Korean works the way it does, and they seldom connect the language to the culture that makes people want to learn it. Korean School combines structured lessons with genuine cultural context and real-world travel language, so you understand what you are saying and have somewhere real to use it. And there is no streak-shaming — your progress is saved, but the pace is yours.

What order should I use the site in?

If you are brand new, start with Hangul and do not skip ahead until you can read the letters — everything else is easier once you can. From there, work through syllable blocks and pronunciation, then move into grammar and vocabulary. Once you can read comfortably, start dipping into the K-Culture guides for listening practice and motivation. The quiz is there whenever you want to check what has stuck.

How much time per day do I need?

Consistency beats intensity. Fifteen to twenty focused minutes a day will take you further than a three-hour session once a week, because language memory is built through repeated, spaced contact. The lessons are deliberately broken into short steps so you can do one or two in a coffee break and still feel you have made progress. Your study streak is tracked to help the habit form — but missing a day is not a failure, it is just a language you are learning for life.
