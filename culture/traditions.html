🏮 Korean Traditions 한국 전통

Explore the festivals, rites of passage, and social customs that have shaped Korean identity for thousands of years.

🎊 Major Korean Holidays
🌙
Chuseok
추석
Korean Harvest Festival / Thanksgiving. Families gather, prepare ancestral rites (차례), and eat 송편 (songpyeon, rice cakes). Held on the 15th day of the 8th lunar month.
🎆
Seollal
설날
Korean Lunar New Year. Children bow (세배) to elders and receive money (세뱃돈). Families eat 떡국 (tteokguk, rice cake soup) to gain a year in age.
🏮
Daeboreum
대보름
The Great Full Moon. People crack nuts with their teeth (부럼) to ward off illness, eat five-grain rice (오곡밥), and light lanterns.
🌸
Children's Day
어린이날
May 5th national holiday. Children receive gifts and parents take them to amusement parks, zoos, and museums.
🧹
Dongji
동지
Winter Solstice. Koreans eat 팥죽 (patjuk, red bean porridge) to ward off evil spirits. The red color is believed to have protective power.
👨‍👩‍👧
Parents' Day
어버이날
May 8th. Children present carnations (카네이션) to parents and grandparents to express gratitude. Schools hold special performances.
🎂 Korean Rites of Passage
👶
Baek-il
백일
100-day celebration of a baby's survival. 백설기 (white rice cake) and 수수경단 (sorghum rice ball) are prepared and shared with neighbors to wish the child health.
🎂
Doljanchi
돌잔치
Baby's 1st birthday. The highlight is 돌잡이 — the baby picks from items (thread, money, books, stethoscope) that predict the child's future.
👘
Gwan-rye
관례
Coming-of-age ceremony. On "Coming of Age Day" (성년의 날, 3rd Monday of May), twenty-year-olds receive roses, perfume, and a first kiss.
💒
Gyeolhon
결혼
Korean Wedding. Combines Western ceremony (white dress, church/hall) with traditional 폐백 — the bride bows to in-laws who toss dates and chestnuts to predict how many children they'll have.
🙏 Korean Social Etiquette
🙇
Bowing (절)
절
A slight bow (15°) for greetings, a deeper bow (45°+) for formal thanks or apologies. Never look away while bowing to an elder.
🤝
Two-Handed Receiving (두 손)
두 손으로 받다
Always receive business cards, gifts, and drinks with two hands or with your right hand supported by your left — shows respect.
🥂
Pouring Drinks (건배)
건배
Never pour your own drink. Pour for others first, especially elders. Hold your glass with two hands when an elder pours for you.
📱
Quiet in Public
조용히 하다
Subway and public spaces are kept quiet. Phone calls should be brief and hushed. Priority seats (노약자석) are always left for elders, pregnant women, and the disabled.
감사합니다.
Gamsahamnida.

죄송합니다.
Joesonghamnida.

💡
눈치 — The Unspoken Social Intelligence
눈치 (nunchi) is the Korean concept of reading the room — sensing what others feel or need without being told. High 눈치 means you know when to speak, when to stay silent, when to pour someone a drink, or when to leave. It's considered one of the most important social skills in Korean culture.
